## 1. The reframe: the brand's core claim is unphotographable

The pasted grammar gives the product promise: **« tenue parfaite 10 jours »**
across a catalogue described as ~90 colours, and the automation thesis names
6 carnations.

    90 colours  ×  6 carnations  ×  10 days  =  5 400 states

A photo shoot can show perhaps twenty. Not for budget reasons — for
**combinatorial** reasons. Which means: *Manucurist has never been able to
prove its own central claim at the scale at which the claim is made.* Every
customer is asked to extrapolate from a hand that is not hers, in a colour
that is not hers, on a day that is not day ten.

This inverts the entire pitch.

| | |
|---|---|
| Old frame | "AI makes your assets cheaper" → a luxury brand hears *cheap* and recoils. Correctly. |
| **New frame** | **"AI makes your promise provable at full scale"** → the brand hears *rigour*. |

Automation stops being a cost argument — which luxury is structurally deaf to —
and becomes an **evidence** argument, which luxury is built on. The 2025 tests
failed the "pas assez luxueux" test partly because the underlying pitch was a
cost pitch wearing a craft costume.

## 2. The moat: luxury is the guarantee of sameness, and sameness is drift control

Every agency in Paris has access to the same generators. Access is not a moat.
The thing no one has is **drift control with receipts**.

A maison's terror about generative imagery is precise and correct: the wrong
green, a skin undertone that shifts between assets, nail geometry that goes
uncanny, a hand that betrays the machine. Luxury *is* the promise of identical
stitching — a house that cannot guarantee its green is the same green across
5 400 assets cannot responsibly use this technology at all.

This repository has been building exactly that guarantee, and it is verified
here, not asserted:

- **Identity-lock / character consistency** — the reference-conditioning
  discipline (never blind-prompt), already named in the pasted recipe §1.
- **Deterministic replay** — same input + same seed ⇒ byte-identical output,
  demonstrated and witnessed in this repo (`test_phase0_replay.py`,
  `test_director_replay.py`, the Daily Verdict golden vectors).
- **Receipts** — every generation hash-bound to its inputs; an asset can be
  reproduced exactly, a year later, from its receipt alone.
- **Hash-convention rigour** — this session found and documented a
  canonicalization schism that would silently corrupt provenance on non-ASCII
  payloads (`FINDING_CANONICALIZATION_SCHISM_V1`). The house that knows *why*
  its hashes could fork is the house whose provenance can be trusted.

**The sellable sentence:** *every image we deliver carries a receipt naming the
reference that locked it, the seed, and the parameters — so you can audit your
own campaign, and regenerate any asset identically in five years.*

No competitor is offering auditable imagery. They are offering pictures.

## 3. The deliverable shift: sell the atelier, not the campaign

The shared-office access is not a convenience — it is a **structural position**
that almost no agency has, and it is being under-used if the output is
campaigns.

    campaign work    per-project fee · recurring pitch risk · client can leave
    THE ATELIER      the brand's own locked generative instrument, built and
                     maintained by UZIK · recurring · switching cost is total

Deliver Manucurist a **machine they press the button on**: locked references,
locked palette (each of the ~90 colours calibrated once, receipted forever),
locked hand grammar, locked light. They generate. The lock holds because UZIK
built and maintains it.

That is why the shared office matters: UZIK is not doing work-for-hire, it is
the atelier that builds the house's instrument. Mutual education — them
teaching standards, UZIK encoding them — is exactly the process that produces
a locked instrument. It is already happening; it is just not yet the product.

## 4. C4 — proposed for the jury: « JOUR DIX » (controlled drift)

Not a replacement for C1–C3, which I have not read. A fourth candidate, from
the reframe above.

**The image:** the same hand, the same colour, day one and day ten. Side by
side. Then the grid: many hands, many colours, all at day ten.

**Why it is the strongest possible demo:** rendering "day ten" convincingly
requires proving you control drift *deliberately* — that you can make a thing
age exactly as much as you decide, and not one shade more. **Controlled drift
is the hardest thing to fake and the easiest thing to verify**, and it is
literally the product claim. C2's insight ("the format proves the machine")
taken one turn further: this format proves the machine *and* the promise in
the same frame.

**The falsifier, stated in advance** (per the operator's own C1 discipline):
if a viewer cannot tell which image is day one and which is day ten, the demo
has failed — it proves nothing about wear. If the day-ten hand looks *degraded*
rather than *intact*, it disproves the product. The target is a narrow band:
visibly ten days later, invisibly worn. **NO_SHIP unless that band is hit.**

That falsifier is the whole test, and it is honest: it can fail.

## 5. Execution ladder (each rung gated, cheap before expensive)

    R0  operator reads this; keeps, composts, or merges C4 into C1/C2/C3
    R1  ONE pair: same hand, same colour, day 1 / day 10. Text-conditioned,
        identity-locked, receipted. Cheap. Answers the only question that
        matters — can we hit the narrow band?
    R2  if R1 lands: one colour × 6 carnations, day 10. Proves the carnation
        axis, the axis photography cannot afford.
    R3  if R2 lands: the grid — enough of 90 × 6 to make 5 400 feel inevitable.
        This is the atelier demo, not a campaign asset.
    R4  side-by-side against the 2025 tests. The « before ». Same agency, same
        thesis, one year of patience, standard reached. That story is worth
        more than the images.
    R5  only now: client. And the ask is the atelier, not the campaign.

Every rung emits a receipt. Every rung can say no. R1 costs almost nothing and
kills the whole thing if it fails — which is the correct order.
