## 1. The thesis (grafted — this is now the design lens)

**Goblin Warren is a playable model of agentic systems.** The surface product
is tending goblins; the real product is a felt intuition for how
semi-autonomous agents behave: direct action vs delegated influence,
signals over commands, specialization, limited attention, memory and drift,
emergence, orchestration over micromanagement.

North star sentence: *"I now understand, in my body, why giving agents too
much autonomy without good signals creates problems — and why shaping the
environment is often more powerful than controlling everything directly."*

HAL note: Level 1 as built already satisfies the updated success criterion —
the completion card's three lines ("You woke the fire yourself / You couldn't
lift the big wood / When you marked it, Bram brought it") ARE the
direct-vs-delegated distinction in the player's own words. No rework needed;
the criterion is retroactively met and test-anchored.

## 2. Ruling on implicit vs explicit (the A/B/C question)

**A during play, B-lite between levels. Never C.**

- In-level: fully implicit. No player-facing words like "agent",
  "orchestration", "stigmergy", "AI". The body learns first. This also obeys
  the standing law: governance/system vocabulary stays off the player surface.
- Between levels: an optional, skippable **fireside reflection** — one card,
  goblin-flavored, icon-first, no score, no shame, "not now" always valid
  (CANNOT-SAY rules apply to quizzes too).
- The pasted quiz set is banked but its language fails the lens it was
  written for — "What does choice B teach you about agentic systems?" is a
  lecture wearing a quiz mask. Rewritten exemplar:

  > ❌ "This is the difference between direct execution and orchestration."
  > ✅ Fireside card: **"Bram brought the wood. Why did he?"**
  >    🔥 Because I ordered him · ✨ Because my mark glowed and he noticed ·
  >    🎲 By chance · 🤷 I'm not sure *(all answers gentle; the second warms
  >    the fire a little — kindness-as-strategy, not grading)*

- A single optional lore codex ("The Warren Book") may name the real
  concepts in plain words for players who go looking. Post-V0.

## 3. Campaign mapping (7 levels hold — 12-level structure banked)

The Grok phase arc (Hook → Play → Depth → Mastery) grafts onto the locked
7-level campaign as a learning-concept column (dev-facing only):

| Level | Scene | Agentic concept (dev-only label) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Dying Fire | direct vs delegated action |
| 2 | The Quiet Signal | agent specialization (Bram vs Lulu attention) |
| 3 | Matcha Morning | care changes agent capacity (kindness as strategy) |
| 4 | The Warren Remembers | statefulness — memory changes behavior |
| 5 | The Night Market | limited attention + resource trade-offs |
| 6 | The Fog Path | drift and delayed consequences |
| 7 | Second Bloom | orchestration — shaping conditions, not commands |

Levels 8–12 (deeper governance/mastery) = post-V0 roadmap, not campaign scope.
Definition of Done stays exactly as gated.

## 4. New pillar grafted: Kindness as Strategy

Helping and caring for goblins produces *mechanical* advantage, never mere
decoration — matcha raises Lulu's exploration range (L3), remembered care
changes future responses (L4), the tending-style ending reflects it (L7).
HAL guard: kindness must never become a currency grind; it is relational and
character-specific (compost verdict already bans matcha-as-buff).

## 5. What this changes right now

Nothing in the shipped Level 1 — it already embodies the thesis. What it
changes going forward: every new mechanic must answer *"which agentic
intuition does this build?"* before it enters a level; anything that can't
answer is composted. Level 2's design goal is sharpened: same mark, two
different goblins, two different reactions — specialization felt, not told.
