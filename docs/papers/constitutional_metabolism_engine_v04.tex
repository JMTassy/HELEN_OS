\section{Boundary-Catalytic Intelligence}

Let each receipt atom be represented as
\[

\]
where $v_i \in \{\text{APPROVE}, \text{REQUEST\_CHANGES}, \text{REJECT}, \text{UNKNOWN}\}$ and $c_i \in [0,1]$ is the confidence.

Define the \textbf{constitutional margin}:
\[
m_i =
\begin{cases}
+c_i & \text{if APPROVE} \\
0   & \text{if REQUEST\_CHANGES or uncertain} \\
-c_i & \text{if confident REJECT}
\end{cases}
\]

The \textbf{boundary weight} is
\[
B_\epsilon(r_i) = \exp\left( -\frac{m_i^2}{2\epsilon^2} \right)
\]
which peaks at the decision boundary ($m_i \approx 0$).

A \textbf{motif} $M$ is a cluster of related receipt atoms. The boundary-catalytic score is
\[
\chi_{\text{BC}}(M) = \chi_0(M) \cdot (1 + \lambda B_\epsilon(M)) \cdot P_{\text{tree}}(M) \cdot \frac{1}{1 + \mu R(M)}
\]
where $\chi_0(M)$ encodes compression, replay stability, witness coherence, and entropy, $P_{\text{tree}}(M)$ is provenance purity, and $R(M)$ is repeller divergence.

This scoring biases future action routing toward high-information boundary motifs.
