## §1. What the bell is

The bell is a physical instance of the **Ghaṇṭākarṇa Mahāvīra
Yantra-Mantra** — a Jain protective talisman tradition.

- **Deity**: *Ghaṇṭākarṇa Mahāvīra* (घंटाकर्ण महावीर)
  - Literal: *"Bell-eared Great Hero"*
  - A Jain yaksha (protective spirit/deity) iconographically depicted
    as a warrior-archer with bow and arrow.
  - The figure visible on the bell's main face matches this
    iconography.
- **Form**: the inscribed mantra plus the central Shatkona (six-pointed
  star) yantra, surrounded by bīja (seed) syllables. This is the
  standard yantric form of the mantra.
- **Function** (per the mantra's own self-description): protection
  against disease, fire, theft, untimely death, snake bite, royal
  oppression, witches, ghosts, demons, and "all diseases" (*sarva-
  vyādhi*).

The poster's observation — *"feels less like a 'bell' and more like a
wizard knowledge device cast into metal"* — is not metaphor. The bell
**is** the protective device by Jain doctrinal definition. Its
ringing is the mantra's vibrational instantiation.

### §2.4 Working English translation

ritual use):

> diseases — svāhā.*

1. **The inscription IS the protection.** Verse 2 says: *"Wherever
   you, O Deity, are inscribed in lines of letters, there diseases

   threats: smallpox, royal authority, witches, ghosts, vetālas,

   The enumeration is the protection schema.

4. **Closure via *svāhā*.** Every formal Vedic/tantric mantra closes
   with *svāhā* ("may it be so" / ritual sealing). The closure is
   structurally required.
