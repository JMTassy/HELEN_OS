## Seal diagrams (structural geometry, not reproduction)

The grimoire's seal designs share a common topology:

1. **Concentric rings** with inscriptions between rings (inner ring =
   names/words of power, outer ring = binding terms)
2. **Cardinal crosses** at N/S/E/W dividing the circle into quadrants
3. **Central figure** (star, cross, or monogram) at the exact center
4. **Material constraint**: "on lead and copper" / "in the hour of
   Saturn" / "on a Good Friday at Midnight" — specific creation
   conditions

This topology is structurally identical to Source Atlas doctrine:
- center = source object
- inner ring = commentary and objections (Talmudic orbit)
- outer ring = references and memory paths
- cardinal axes = the four governance quadrants
- material constraint = the receipt (creation conditions are recorded)

The Coat Ride seal adds one more element: "stand in the middle, and do
not step your feet outside this symbol." This is CONTAINMENT — the
operator stands inside the boundary, not outside it. The operator is
not observing the system; the operator is within the system, bounded
by the same membrane that bounds the entities.

## CHIDDUSH (the compressed original insight)

**BOUNDARY → CREDENTIAL → FORMAT → CONTENT → BIND**

Both Faust and HELEN converge on this ordering because it is forced
by cost minimization. Each stage is cheaper than the next. Reversing
any pair either wastes computation or risks binding an unauthorized
result.

The convergence evidence is the chiddush — not the ordering itself
(which HELEN already implements) but the fact that an independent
16th-century protocol arrives at the same ordering for the same
structural reasons. The grimoire is a witness, not a source.

**Additional chiddush from the visual pages:**

The seal topology (concentric rings with cardinal axes and central
figure) is structurally identical to Source Atlas layout. This

density decreases from center to periphery, authority decreases from
center to periphery, and the axes provide orientation (which quadrant

in a bounded 2D space converges on this radial layout.
